\section{Governance and indexation}
\label{sec:governance}

\subsection{Three classes of constant}

\paragraph{Genesis data.} Fixed when the community is created and never amendable: the
chain id, the root salt, and the validator floor --- how few validators any removal may
leave standing, $\kConstMinValidatorsRealChain$ for a real chain and $\kConstMinValidators$
for the harness one. Governing the first two would defeat what they establish: a chain id
that could change would stop separating one network's signatures from another's, and a
salt that could change would stop protecting the preimages already committed under it.
The floor is the ceremony's because only the ceremony knows which kind of chain it is
founding, and the ledger holds it afterwards, because a floor the ceremony alone held
binds the founding and nothing after it: a four becomes a three on one validator's free
exit, and a one after three, on no vote at all.

Two \emph{sets} are also seeded at genesis, and neither is frozen: somebody has to be
first. The validator set's initial composition is genesis data, but voting power is
amendable at runtime by proposal, which is how a community replaces an institution that
has left or misbehaved without starting a new chain. The initial underwriters are genesis
data in the same sense: a supply rises thereafter only by an endorsed amendment
(\S\ref{sec:seed-amendments}), and any member may lower one. A declaration confers
nothing on the declarer and a coalition cannot underwrite itself
(Corollary~\ref{cor:self-uw}); what it does is accept liability.

\paragraph{Governed constants.} Amendable within static safe ranges by proposal and
assent. There are six: the client-advisory risk midpoint, the visibility policy, the
operation-bond fraction, the stake decay ratio, the seed-amendment rate $\beta$
(\S\ref{sec:seed-amendments}), and the insured horizon --- how far past its acceptance a
claim may mature and still hold a reservation (\S\ref{sec:recourse}). Every one is
\emph{dimensionless}, which is what lets a static safe-range table stay exact across
re-denomination: a denomination-valued constant would drift out of its own constitutional
range the first time the unit was rescaled, while the range still read the same numbers.

\paragraph{Frozen constants.} Amendable by nothing. The adoption threshold
$\Theta_{\mathrm{adopt}}$, genesis $\kConstThetaAdopt$, is the mass an assenting coalition
needs to enact an amendment --- including one targeting the threshold itself --- and
$\Theta_{\mathrm{val}}$, genesis $\kConstThetaAdoptValidator$ --- two thirds, to the last
bit a double carries --- is the mass it needs to change who orders the ledger.

\subsection{Amendments and adoption}

\paragraph{Assenting mass.} The weight of an assenting coalition is its share of the
community's \emph{external} seed:
\[
  \mathrm{mass}(A) \;=\; \frac{\sum_{u \in A} \mathrm{ext}(u)}{\sum_{u} \mathrm{ext}(u)},
\]
where $\mathrm{ext}(u)$ is the part of $u$'s declared supply that arrived through a
ceremony --- genesis, or a seed amendment the community endorsed. Every other candidate
was measured and failed: a headcount is a count of free signatures, and a cut over the
assenters counts internal declarations at face value and measures who the seed
\emph{reaches} rather than who put it up --- a founder who declares the whole seed and
backs one member for all of it has capacity zero, and that member alone carries the vote.
The external seed is the one quantity in this system that a signature cannot manufacture,
Assumption~\ref{ass:free-signatures} applied to votes instead of to credit. Members may
propose --- the floor is only that the proposer has something to lose --- and an assent
from outside the electorate is refused rather than recorded at zero. Mass is monotone in
$A$ by construction, so assenting can only ever help.

Members assent; when assenting mass reaches the threshold for its kind, the proposal
enacts. There are six kinds and one measure, and two thresholds:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item a \textbf{parameter change}, naming one governed constant and a value;
  \item a \textbf{re-denomination}, the atomic change of unit below;
  \item \textbf{suspension} and \textbf{reinstatement} of a member --- the only sanction
        the community votes on, as against the arithmetic one of
        Property~\ref{prop:forfeit}. It revokes \emph{origination}, not standing: a
        suspended member may still discharge what they owe, be paid, net a sale against
        debts they already carry, extend an existing obligation with the creditor's own
        signature, accept relief from a supporter, lower a declared supply, and register
        guardians or rotate keys --- none of those is a new obligation, shutting the first
        would be a debtors' prison, closing the supply door would make the sanctioned a
        permanent involuntary insurer, and closing the last would let a sanction about
        conduct strand a member with a stolen key. One proposal stays open to a suspended
        member, to write and to vote on: reinstatement, and nothing else, so that an
        electorate cannot sanction itself out of existence (below). What suspension does
        close, beyond origination, is the member's own write budget: a non-active account
        confers as before but has no headroom of its own, so what remains open to it is
        paid for by whoever else signs;
  \item a \textbf{validator power}, which sets one member's voting power and removes them
        at zero;
  \item a \textbf{seed amendment} (\S\ref{sec:seed-amendments}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Changing who orders the ledger takes two thirds.} A validator power, and a
suspension or reinstatement whose target currently holds voting power, need
$\Theta_{\mathrm{val}}$ rather than $\Theta_{\mathrm{adopt}}$. The distinction is
recoverability: a constant moved too far is moved back through the same door, and every
value inside a constitutional range is one the ledger keeps working at, while a coalition
that holds the validator set decides which blocks exist, including the ones that would
undo it. At one threshold for every kind, a coalition holding half the seed removes every
other validator down to the ledger's floor and suspends whoever objects, alone and in one
epoch. Two thirds is the bar the consensus below already uses: a quorum is $2f+1$ of
$3f+1$, so a set changeable by less than two thirds is changeable by a minority the
protocol already tolerates as faulty. Whether the higher bar applies is read off the
target's current voting power rather than off the proposal's kind, because suspension
removes a validator too and a rule keyed on the word would leave the same door open one
name over; the reading is a function of committed state, so every node agrees which bar
applies.

The first two kinds carry a cooldown of $\kConstGovCooldownEpochs$ epochs --- per constant
for a parameter change, one for re-denomination --- which bounds how fast a dial can be
walked. It is checked before the enacting assent is recorded, because the state machine
has no rollback: a refusal that had already banked the assent would let a later one enact
on a support set including somebody who had been told they were refused.

One measure decides all six because there is almost nothing to leave on a lighter tier.
A bond fraction at its ceiling is censorship by arithmetic; a stake decay at either end of
its range is a credit freeze enacted as a rounding rule; an insured horizon at its floor
is the same freeze enacted as a term; $\beta$ governs how fast the electorate itself may
grow; and the visibility policy, flipped, unseals every amount in a community that had
chosen to seal them. Five of the six governed constants take something from members who
did not vote; the sixth is a client-side score constant no consensus rule reads, and a
second measure for that one constant would be two rules for one act, in exchange for
keeping alive a measure the paragraph above rules out.

\begin{property}[Safe-range soundness]
\label{prop:saferange}
A value outside a constant's safe range cannot be enacted, whatever mass supports it.
The state machine refuses it at application, so the range is a property of the ledger
rather than of the proposers' restraint.
\end{property}

The ranges are bounded on \emph{both} sides, and for the bond fraction and the decay
ratio the ceiling is the load-bearing half, for the reasons above: a hostile amendment
must be loud and attributable rather than economic.

\subsection{The constitutional freeze}

\begin{property}[The door cannot amend its own guard]
\label{prop:freeze}
No governed constant is $\Theta_{\mathrm{adopt}}$, and no amendment can write to it. The
freeze is enforced by omission: the key space of amendable constants has no variant
naming it, so no proposal can be constructed that targets it.
\end{property}

Without this, a coalition holding the threshold could lower it and then adopt anything at
the lowered bar, so the guarantee that amendment requires broad assent would be exactly
one amendment deep.

\subsection{Seed amendments}
\label{sec:seed-amendments}

A supply is written by a ceremony and by nothing else (\S\ref{sec:standing}), which keeps
a free identity from declaring a billion --- and, with genesis the only ceremony, would
close the ledger's external-commitment door for everybody: a founder could never raise
their supply, a newcomer bringing backing from outside would have no transition to arrive
through, and a group forming on an established ledger in year three could never have
underwriters at all. Genesis fixes two sets, the validator half is amendable at runtime,
and the credit half must be too.

No set of signatures can prove externality (Assumption~\ref{ass:free-signatures}), so
there is nothing to verify: genesis was never a verification but a \emph{ceremony} the
ledger recorded, humans checking each other out of band with the file as the record. The
repair is to make the ceremony repeatable, and a \emph{seed amendment} is that: an
ordinary proposal, carried by ordinary assent, whose effect is to add its amount to the
proposer's declared supply outside the capacity cap. Three properties make it safe to
have at all.

\paragraph{The proposer is the beneficiary, structurally.} A supply is a standing consent
to inherit the debts of those the community's stakes reach through the underwriter
(\S\ref{sec:recourse}), so an amendment naming somebody \emph{else} would volunteer a
member to underwrite, and every discharge here is authorised by the party who loses if it
is wrong. The proposal kind carries no beneficiary field: its beneficiary is its author,
so the consent is the author's own signature and cannot be omitted. The author may not
assent their own amendment, because assenting mass is a share of the external seed and an
amendment adds external seed to its author: without the exclusion, an underwriter would
vote their own raise through on the strength of the very quantity the raise enlarges, each
one carrying the next more easily.

\paragraph{The rate is bounded, and bounded on the right base.} Amendments enacted in one
epoch may add at most $\beta$ times the seed the epoch opened with, and the headroom does
not carry over between epochs. Every unit of every supply arrived through a ceremony
(\S\ref{sec:standing}), so the roll \emph{is} that seed; a rule that admitted
declarations against conferred capacity would give the base an inflatable second half and
turn the same multiple into amendment headroom (\S\ref{sec:standing} measures it).

\paragraph{Zero stays absorbing.} A community that underwrote nothing at genesis has an
external seed of zero, and $\beta$ times zero is zero, so no amendment can ever be
admitted --- and it has no assenting denominator either, since the denominator \emph{is}
the external seed, so no proposal of any kind can be enacted.
Assumption~\ref{ass:free-signatures} is untouched: a repeatable ceremony still needs a
seed to be a fraction of.

The bound changes the \emph{shape} of the residual risk without removing it. A coalition
holding $\Theta_{\mathrm{adopt}}$ of the external seed can still assent to a commitment
that does not exist; no rule prevents a community from lying to itself, and none ever
will. The bound turns that from an explosion into a slow, public, attributable leak ---
measured at $4.3\times$ over thirty epochs with $\beta$ pinned to its constitutional
ceiling and every amendment the community could pass being passed --- during which the
only parties at risk are lenders, who must still choose to deliver real goods against the
inflated figure, and who can simply stop. The same humans were trusted absolutely at
genesis; the mechanism adds no trust assumption, it makes the existing one repeatable and
auditable.

\paragraph{What making the seed the electorate exposes.} Three things, recorded rather
than mitigated. Ordinary members have no vote: that is the decision itself --- the premise
given up is that credit trust implies a governance mandate, and the scene above is why
there is no sound capacity-based member vote to keep instead; a community that wants to
widen its electorate does it the way it grants credit, by endorsing a real commitment.
The electorate is a specific, listed set of accounts, so the constitution's soft underbelly
is the \emph{guardian sets of the external underwriters}: key recovery
(\S\ref{sec:implementation}) is opt-in, self-declared and veto-windowed, but a threshold
of an underwriter's own guardians seizes a vote along with an account, and narrowing that
would be a rule about who may be trusted with a key, which is not a rule this design knows
how to write. And suspension silences a voter without removing their weight from the
seed, so a coalition at the ordinary bar could suspend everyone else and withdraw, leaving
an electorate in which nothing could ever be enacted again --- the argument that a
constant is recoverable through its door assumes the electorate survives its own
sanctions. So the sanctioned keep one vote, on reinstatement: a majority of the seed cannot
be held suspended, which is what a majority of the seed means, and a minority still cannot
reinstate itself.

Finally, an amendment endorses something concrete: a substitution liability
(\S\ref{sec:recourse}), enforceable, and failing in public if it was never real.
Amendments without substitution would endorse nothing.

\subsection{Re-denomination}

Prices in the community unit drift against the outside economy. The response is
\emph{re-denomination}: an atomic, governed change of unit by a factor $\pi$, bounded per
event by $|\ln \pi| \le \kConstRedenomBandLn$.

\begin{theorem}[Exactness up to the minor unit]
\label{thm:redenominate}
Re-denomination by $\pi$ multiplies every denomination-valued quantity --- outstanding
amounts, stakes, reservations, bond encumbrances, the reference denomination --- by $\pi$
simultaneously, and leaves every dimensionless quantity unchanged. Consequently every
member's capacity and every ratio of capacity to debt are preserved exactly in the reals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Capacity is a maximum flow over $\free = \stake - \res$. Scaling every $\stake$ and
$\res$ by $\pi$ scales every cut by $\pi$, hence the minimum cut by $\pi$, hence
capacity by $\pi$. Outstanding amounts scale identically, so each comparison of amount
against capacity has both sides scaled by the same positive factor and its outcome is
unchanged.
\end{proof}

The theorem is why stakes and reservations must be in the rescale set: a re-denomination
that moved the constants and the debts but not the stake graph would silently reprice
every credit limit in the community while appearing to be a change of unit.

\paragraph{What ``exactly'' cannot mean at the minor unit.} The capacity path is
integer --- every stake, supply and reserved arc is a whole number of minor units --- and
$\pi$ is rational rather than integral, so the products are not. Every one is rounded
\emph{down}, the only safe direction, since rounding a stake up would let a reservation
claim standing that nothing carries. Three consequences follow, and the implementation
states them where the rounding happens.

An insured obligation \emph{owes exactly what it holds}: its amount is re-derived from
its re-denominated arcs rather than scaled on its own, so it rounds down by at most one
minor unit per arc. Scaling the amount independently would put $\lfloor \sum \rfloor$
against $\sum \lfloor\,\rfloor$, and \S\ref{sec:verification}'s reservation invariant
asks for equality, not closeness.

The reservations are a \emph{flow}, not a list, and one supply arc may carry several
paths. The supply side therefore rounds down once where the stake arcs beneath it round
down separately, so after the flooring each debtor's supply claim is brought back to what
their arcs can still deliver --- per debtor, because it is the union of a debtor's holds
that is an augmentation, and a subrogated claim holds a share of each arc rather than a
path of its own (\S\ref{sec:recourse}).

An obligation smaller than one minor unit holds nothing, and an obligation that holds
nothing is not insured, so a downward re-denomination can move a sub-minor claim into the
uninsured tier. The debt itself is untouched; what it loses is a reservation that had
already rounded to zero.
