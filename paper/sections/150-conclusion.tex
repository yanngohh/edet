\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A credit community must decide how much to stand behind each of its members, and every
mechanism for deciding this tends toward the same failure: the decision becomes a
process, the process accumulates rules, and the rules accumulate exceptions until nobody
can say what the system guarantees.

This design makes the decision an equation. Capacity is the maximum flow from a
community's underwriters to an account, over directed stakes that creditors have placed
and that nobody can place beyond what they may confer, with outstanding credit reserving
the flow that justified it, and an underwriter is any member who has declared a liability
they will stand behind. Admission, reputation, credit limits and Sybil resistance are that
one quantity, and the security results follow from max-flow/min-cut rather than from a
policy anybody has to enforce: identities are free and worthless, fabricated trade is
arithmetically void rather than detected, a colluding member passes on exactly what they
hold, and no configuration of identities, volumes, timing or collusion converts real
commitment into more credit than it is worth, so attacking costs what participating costs
(\S\ref{sec:security}).

What it costs: a newcomer needs somebody willing to take a first uninsured risk, and no
mechanism supplies that willingness; capacity can fall through no fault of its holder,
being a claim on others' present willingness rather than a possession; allocation under
contention is first-come-first-served, so the total is bounded correctly while who gets it
is decided by ordering; and the medium is not money --- not accepted by strangers, not a
store of value across decades, not saveable by anybody acting alone.

The boundary this design does not cross is economic, and narrower than the one usually
drawn here. It guarantees that a community's medium cannot be inflated by an adversary,
that its books balance, that its losses are bounded by what its members agreed to bear,
and that its unit can be re-denominated without disturbing a single real position. It does
not have to make anybody want to \emph{hold} the medium, because nobody holds it: a sale
discharges before it creates, so a member who owes is paid by ceasing to owe. What it
cannot supply is the condition that makes this true --- enough of what a community buys
being available from within it --- a fact about the community, knowable before anybody
founds anything. Nor can it seed a community that underwrote nothing at genesis: zero is
absorbing, and the ceremony is the only door.
