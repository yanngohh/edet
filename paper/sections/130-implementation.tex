\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{One node binary, and a wallet}

A validator runs one program --- a Byzantine-fault-tolerant consensus engine, the
replicated state machine, a write-ahead log and an HTTP surface for reads and submission,
in a single binary with no sequencer, relay or indexer --- small enough for a community
institution to run without specialists. Validators are the community's charter
institutions rather than a market: the validator set drives both quorum and proposer
selection, so a runtime change to it must reach the wire and not only the ledger.

A member's device runs a \emph{wallet}. Keys and signing stay local; the client reads and
submits over the HTTP surface of a node its member chose from a declared \emph{set} of
node URLs rather than one, since a cross-check whose peer list comes from the party being
checked is circular. The node publishes the state commitment, so a client can put the same
question to several nodes and compare: a node can withhold or omit, and cannot forge. A
handset is not asked to validate --- it sleeps, roams and is reclaimed at the operating
system's pleasure, none of which a quorum should depend on.

The client's own acceptance rule (\S\ref{sec:standing}) is signing, so it decides for
exactly as long as the client is \emph{running}: in front of the member, or behind, where
the client holds its page up against the platform's own attempt to pause it --- a tray the
window hides into, and on Android a foreground service under a re-resumed view. For no
longer than that. Nothing outside that page holds the key, so a closed app signs nothing,
and neither the service nor the tray can decide anything on its own. A device left
unplugged and stationary with its screen off has its network suspended by the system,
which the client documents rather than fights: it acquires no wake lock, and the exemption
is the member's to grant in their own settings.

Consensus is \emph{more} necessary under this model than under a purely subjective one:
capacity is a conserved budget over a shared graph, and the total order decides who lends
(Remark~\ref{rem:order}).

\subsection{Feeless writes}

Writes are free at the point of use (Property~\ref{prop:no-rent}). A member has a
per-epoch allowance of $\kConstBondFreeAllowance$ transitions; beyond it a transition
encumbers a refundable bond of $\kConstBondUnitFraction \cdot \vbase$ against their own
write headroom, released after $\kConstBondReleaseEpochs$ epoch
(\S\ref{sec:recourse}). The allowance is not a per-key entitlement: it goes to accounts
the community has put something behind, because keys are free and $N$ keys would
otherwise carry $N$ allowances --- Assumption~\ref{ass:free-signatures} one layer below
credit. A seated row does hold one self-act its seat paid for --- registering guardians,
once, on its own key --- spent by the gate whether or not the write is accepted, and by a
refusal alike; seats are bounded by the seed, so it is a stock and never a rate.

\begin{property}[The write floor is measured against the seed's reach]
\label{prop:write-floor}
Write headroom is the maximum flow of \S\ref{sec:standing} computed with each
underwriter's source arc carrying only the part a ceremony seated, plus the member's own
such part, \emph{gross of outstanding credit}, net of what they owe and have already
encumbered. It is \emph{not} the declared supply.
\end{property}

\paragraph{Gross of outstanding credit, and only the member's own debt nets.} A credit
limit is netted because a unit of standing backs one obligation at a time
(Def.~\ref{def:reservation}); a write floor is what a member has \emph{to lose}, and the
only netting it needs is the subtraction the property names. Reading the reach on the
residual graph subtracts the member's own debt twice --- their obligation reserves the
arcs into them, and what they owe comes off again --- and everybody else's once: a member
reached for $500$ who borrowed $200$ reads $100$ of headroom where the property says
$300$, and at $20\%$ community utilisation a member who has borrowed nothing loses their
whole floor and allowance because another member drew the underwriter that reaches them;
drawn to its ceiling, such a community stops trading altogether, where a ceiling that
binds withholds \emph{insurance} (\S\ref{sec:standing}) and the trade it refuses to insure
is the trade that dissolves the bootstrap. The qualification the allowance turns on is the
same reading taken gross --- what the community has put behind the account, not how much
of it is unspent this afternoon --- with an open default holding the allowance until it is
cured, since a default consuming the defaulter's standing is the sanction.

The distinction from the declared supply is load-bearing. A declaration is a promise made
on the strength of another promise (\S\ref{sec:standing}), so a chain of them inflates
geometrically: measured on six founders at $2{,}500$ with a single honest $300$ trade
behind the entire chain, write headroom read from declarations \textbf{doubles with every
accomplice} --- $19{,}200$ at seven, $614{,}400$ at twelve --- while the real cut over the
whole coalition is $300$ throughout; accounts being free, that is an unbounded channel
rather than an amplification, and it makes the traffic bound vacuous. Read from the seed's
reach, every member of that coalition holds exactly $300$, so $k$ colluding accounts hold
what $k$ honestly-backed members hold: attacking the write surface costs what
participating costs, the same standing this design claims everywhere
(\S\ref{sec:security}).

A supply \emph{reduction} is free, because a member must never be priced out of reducing
what they stand behind; nobody may raise one (\S\ref{sec:standing}), and restating an
unchanged supply is bonded like any transition that shrinks nothing. Every free class must
answer ``how many can one member force in an epoch?'', since a free transition is
unlimited and each admitted transaction is recorded against replay: a partial discharge or
cure is at least a $\kConstMaxInstallments$th of the original, so a row is discharged in
at most that many payments and the closing one --- an uninsured amount is bounded by
nothing but the ingress ceiling, so without the floor one allowance slot buys $2^{51}$
free payments of one minor unit, each a durable replay id and, on an insured row, a
re-hold on every validator; transfer and exit close what they touch; the permissionless
cranks are idempotent and forget the id of a call that found nothing to do; an assent or
an arbiter's attestation is one per pair, the second refused.

Refusal is a write too. A free class refused at dispatch is priced by one allowance slot
of the first signer in canonical order who has one, and where nobody present has one it
leaves nothing behind: the signed intents whose success is a matter of timing --- an exit
while owing, a lowering below the floor --- keep their id where somebody could pay for it,
so the identical bytes replayed once they would succeed are refused, while a refusal
nobody can pay for is not a durable write. The three permissionless cranks carry no
signature to defer and always leave nothing; they are one list, and the node's ingress
asks the same list to decide what may arrive unsigned.

What justifies a zero is whatever refuses the \emph{second} call, not what the first
accomplishes: an \textsc{Exit} that returns success on an account that has already left is
admitted $500$ of $500$ times with no allowance and no headroom. The replay record is why
it matters. It is durable, committed state kept for a whole transaction lifetime, so
\emph{recording one is itself a write}, and a write no bond prices is the attack the
schedule exists to refuse. The gate's order is therefore load-bearing --- charge, then
record, then dispatch: a transaction refused at the gate was never admitted and leaves
nothing behind; one refused at dispatch has been attempted against the ledger's economic
state, and spends its identifier so that a signature cannot be held until the same attempt
would succeed.

Account creation escapes the question by not being a transition. It cannot be bonded on
its own --- a key that is not yet a member has no headroom to encumber, and a creation
somebody had to pay for would be a creation somebody could refuse --- and an unbonded
transition is bounded by nothing, so the most it could carry is a ledger-wide per-epoch
cap, which is a censorship lever rather than a quota: whoever fills it first shuts
onboarding for everyone until the boundary, at no cost. An account row is therefore seated
by the first \emph{bonded trade} that names its key (\S\ref{sec:standing}). Creation stays
unapprovable, because the trade decides whether the row appears and nobody else does, and
it remains inert (Corollary~\ref{cor:sybil}), which is why nothing needs approving.

\paragraph{A write budget bounds a rate; a row is a stock.} That the trade was bonded
bounds how fast rows appear and not how many: a bond is reserved and released, so what it
buys over time is unbounded, while a row is permanent and hashed into every state root.
Measured, a row that can seat on its own headroom carries a farm behind a single honest
edge of $500$ from $91$ rows after six epochs at a wash of $40$ to $1{,}120$ at $100$, and
raising the establishment floor tenfold leaves every count identical: the floor withholds
the \emph{allowance} a seated row would hold, never the row.

\begin{property}[A row is a stock and holds its price]
\label{prop:seat}
Seating a row reserves one bond unit of flow from the community's seed to the sponsor,
over the same stake graph credit runs on, on a second reservation map credit never reads
--- the sponsor's own supply arc included --- and no transition releases it.
\end{property}

Both halves are load-bearing. Held per account, the price is the same backing read once
per child, so a farm behind one edge compounds; held on the shared graph, a child's
residual is the cut less what its parent and siblings already drew. Released on any
schedule, the price is a rate again --- a seat shrinking with decay is renewed at $2.3\%$
of the backing per epoch by an accomplice's free trades --- and never released, the count
is bounded by backing alone (Cor.~\ref{cor:seats}). What it costs: $25$ rows behind one
edge of $500$, $250$ behind $5{,}000$, unchanged over $400$ epochs of daily renewal, and
$+25$ for a second backer of $500$. A community seats $\sum \supply$ over the bond unit
and then nobody --- the honest preset's seed of $7{,}500$ carries $375$ rows --- until a
ceremony raises the seed, which is the door every supply arc already comes through.

The sponsor's own supply arc is in the query, the one place the write layer must not read
credit's rule: a cut into an account excludes that account's own declaration, because
nobody underwrites their own borrowing, so a founding underwriter's capacity is zero, and
a community whose first members nobody could seat would not start.

Three things bend, and are stated rather than worked around. The seat is the one bond no
transition returns, because what it bought does not go away by a transition; only the
sweep retiring an empty row returns it (\S\ref{sec:standing}), which keeps a community
with ordinary churn from holding its dead at its ceiling. The ceiling is real: at
$\sum \supply$ over the bond unit nobody seats until a ceremony, and a bound that never
binds is not one. And the reservation does not reroute earlier seats, so a seat can be
refused that a full re-solve would have fitted --- the first-come conservatism the insured
tier already carries, and never a seat the bound forbids.

Measured against the ordinary acceptance beside it, the seat adds about a fifth: the same
trade naming a key that already has a row against one that does not reads $29.7$\,ms and
$35.5$\,ms at $20{,}000$ accounts, $1.03$\,ms and $1.18$\,ms at $1{,}000$, both conditions
interleaved in one sitting --- less than the two full queries its shape suggests, because
both stop at one bond unit rather than computing a maximum.

\subsection{Determinism}

Capacity is consensus-critical: replicas that compute it differently disagree about
whether a transaction is valid. Three commitments make it reproducible.

\paragraph{Every amount is an integer.} Stakes, reservations, contract amounts, cached
debt, bond encumbrance and arbitration attestations are held in minor units,
$\kConstMinor$ per unit of denomination, and the maximum-flow computation is exact
integer arithmetic. Floating point is \emph{structurally} unsafe here, not merely
imprecise: in the flow search, two replicas comparing residual capacities against an
epsilon can agree on the resulting value and still take different augmenting paths,
leaving different reservations behind; in the book, a cached total maintained by addition
and subtraction drifts from the same total recounted, so an audit comparing the two must
either demand an exactness the quantities cannot deliver or admit a tolerance, and a
tolerance is a claim that the cache and the book may legitimately differ, which they may
not. Real numbers cross the boundary in exactly two places: a transaction's payload on the
way in, and a served view on the way out.

The boundary has a ceiling, and it sits where the crossing stops being exact rather than
where the integer type gives out. A payload's amount is a float, so a view leaving and the
payment that closes the row against it make the round trip twice --- two roundings, hence
a discrepancy of at most $2^{-52}$ of the amount, under half a minor unit only below
$2^{51}$; above that a debtor paying the exact figure the ledger served them is told they
have over-paid. So an amount over $\kConstMaxAmountMinor$ minor units is refused at every
ingress, and refused \emph{before} the conversion, since rounding a real number to an
integer clamps at both ends and a clamp is not a refusal.

\paragraph{Iteration order is a function of state.} The flow network is built from
ordered maps in key order, so adjacency order depends on the ledger's contents and never
on the order records arrived.

\paragraph{The augmenting search is iterative.} A recursive walk is bounded only by the
level graph's depth, which is bounded only by the account count, and a stack overflow
inside block validation is a liveness fault, so the search maintains its own path stack.

\subsection{Cost}

There are two costs, and only one of them is a capacity query.

\paragraph{Per acceptance.} Booking an obligation runs exactly one query, limit-capped:
an acceptance asks whether room exists for one amount rather than for the maximum. It
costs $0.17$\,ms at $1{,}000$ accounts, $2.9$\,ms at $10{,}000$, $7.1$\,ms at
$20{,}000$ with $160{,}000$ stakes and $36$\,ms at $100{,}000$ with $800{,}000$; the
read side --- an account's published capacity --- asks for the maximum, at $0.28$, $4.6$,
$12$ and $104$\,ms. Early termination is worth $1.6$ to $2.9$ times, an implementation
detail rather than a strategy.

Most of an acceptance is the BUILD rather than the search --- two thirds at $1{,}000$
accounts and nine tenths at $100{,}000$, since an early exit stops long before the search
has seen the network the build had to write; of a full cut the build is a third. The
residual network is constructed fresh on every query, so it holds no state that can drift
from the ledger. A persistent adjacency beside the edge map is not taken, and not for
determinism --- held in key order, a patched adjacency and a rebuilt one are one structure
a probe can hold equal --- but for what it buys: a second structure every write to the
edge map must keep equal to the first, in exchange for the build's share of an early-exit
query on a term that bounds nothing, since an acceptance is paid per transaction and the
full cuts, which gain a third at most, are the ones the memo below keeps off the block.
What is taken is the constant factors that need no second structure: the adjacency is a
compressed row rather than a vector per node and the node index is counted rather than
sorted, together a factor of three on the same term, measured A/B on one machine; and the
reservation on each edge is read by one walk of the reservation map in the edge map's own
order rather than a lookup per edge, without which the build grows with how much of the
graph is reserved --- from $4.4$ to $20$\,ms at $20{,}000$ accounts between no edge
reserved and every edge reserved, and from $33$ to $81$\,ms at $100{,}000$, against $5.6$
and $44$\,ms with every edge reserved once the map is walked.

\paragraph{Per block.} Every validator re-audits the invariants of
\S\ref{sec:verification} after every committed block, and the cut bound is evaluated over
sets: all insured debtors together, and one set per underwriter carrying any of them.
Computing that family costs $U + 3$ \emph{full} queries whatever the block contained,
where $U$ is the number of underwriters with live insured debt --- $4$\,ms, $464$\,ms and
$2.13$\,s for $1{,}000$, $10{,}000$ and $20{,}000$ accounts with $U = n/100$, two orders
of magnitude above the acceptance query and, at the top of that range, more than a whole
consensus round. That is what the definition costs. A validator does not pay it, for two
separate reasons, and the second is the one that matters at an epoch boundary.

\textbf{The invariant must hold over the whole family after every block; it does not
follow that every set must be recomputed after every block.} A cut is a maximum flow over
$\free$ with pristine reservations, so it is a pure function of the stake edges, the
declared supplies and the id space, and monotone in all three: raising an edge, raising a
supply or admitting more accounts can only raise a maximum flow. A cut this audit has
already verified therefore stays a valid \emph{lower bound} until one of those three
falls, and a draw within that bound closes the clause with no query at all; only a bound
that fails to cover the draw costs a real one. What can lower them is a short structural
list rather than a hope about traffic: Definition~\ref{def:stake} is a peak rule, so
settlement --- the only writer of the graph --- can only raise an edge, and the whole of
the other direction is decay at the epoch boundary, a governed re-denomination, and a
supply reduction. So a block that changed nothing economic computes \textbf{no} cut at
all, and a block drawing new insured credit through one underwriter computes \textbf{two}
--- the set of all insured debtors, which gained a member, and that one underwriter's.

That argument is worth exactly its premise, and the premise fails once a day.
\textbf{Decay lowers every edge free to fall}, so the epoch-advance block finds the
monotone argument gone and pays the whole family: $201$ cuts after decay against the
$201$ a cold audit computes. The memo buys nothing on the one block that was already the
odd one out.

\paragraph{The second reason: an obligation is a witness.} Invariant~\ref{inv:capacity}
asks whether a set's drawn credit is within its cut, and a cut is a \emph{maximum} flow,
so a feasible flow of that value settles the clause without producing the maximum. Every
insured obligation already stores one: exactly which arcs it drew and how much on each
(Remark~\ref{rem:charge-supply}). Feasibility is three conditions, and the audit checks
two of them anyway --- arc usage within the stake (invariant 4) and supply usage within
the declaration (invariant 5); the third, that the stored hold is a \emph{flow} at all ---
what arrives at a node leaves it --- is one pass over the arcs the obligations hold, with
no query in it. \textbf{Decay cannot touch a witness}: invariant 4 floors every edge at its
live reservation, so whatever the epoch did, the arcs the holds occupy are still there.
The memo dies at the boundary and the witness does not, which is precisely where the cost
was.

Measured (\texttt{just cost}), both arms on the same state and adjacent in time --- the
cold definition, which computes every set, against the commit path a validator runs:

$94\times$ at the top of the range, and the two cuts still computed are
invariant~6's forward-and-reversed determinism pair rather than the cut bound, which
computes none. The family has not shrunk --- the invariant still holds over all $201$ sets
after every block --- and neither has the theorem; a stored proof settles it instead of a
computation.

\paragraph{The audit is not the only whole-ledger pass a block pays for.} A block also
commits to the ledger, and a full build of that commitment re-encodes and re-hashes
every row. The tree is incremental instead (the state root, below): a leaf's salt binds
the EPOCH rather than the block, so an unchanged record keeps its leaf hash for an epoch
and an ordinary block rehashes the rows it wrote and the paths above them. Measured as the
pair in one sitting on one machine, over the shape a wash farm produces --- one account
and one in-edge per seat --- the definition's full build against what a block costs
through the incremental one: $1.1$\,ms against $0.007$\,ms at $1{,}000$ rows, $6.1$
against $0.009$ at $10{,}000$, $30.6$ against $0.012$ at $50{,}000$, $73.6$ against
$0.015$ at $100{,}000$. The definition is linear in the ledger and the block is flat in
it; what the block scales with is the OUT-DEGREE of the accounts it wrote to, because a
stake row is one leaf per creditor, so writing an edge re-encodes that creditor's own row
and nothing else.

One full build remains, at the epoch boundary, where every salt changes --- the block
that already rewrites every member row, decays every stake and sweeps the contract book,
so it costs nothing the boundary was not paying. What the epoch-bound salt buys is a
bounded disclosure rather than none: within one epoch an unchanged record keeps its leaf
hash, so a member holding two of their own proofs from that epoch learns from the sibling
hashes whether the record adjacent to theirs in identifier order changed between the two
heights; across epochs nothing links, and a proof is served only to its subject or to a
validator, who holds the ledger anyway.

No working copy is kept per block. The invariant audit is a fail-stop whose depth is the
log append that happens after the audit: a refused block is not on disk, and the node that
refused it stops, commits nothing further and answers every read with the height it
stopped at and why. A copy to roll back to is a second whole-ledger pass per block for the
window between the refusal and the process exiting --- $0.1$, $1.2$, $17.6$ and
$19.0$\,ms across the same four sizes in the same sitting --- and what remains linear per
block is the audit's own structural walks.

The witness is the data and never a flag: the audit compares the present edges and
supplies against its own snapshot element by element, because a transition that lowered an
edge is exactly what the audit exists to catch. None of it can change a verdict --- it
decides only whether a dominated computation runs --- so a validator resuming from a
snapshot starts cold, does more work, and halts on exactly the states a warm one halts on.
The order the two are consulted in is load-bearing: asked before the memo, the witness
runs on every audit and an ordinary block pays for a pass it did not need ($27$\,ms
against $67$\,ms at $20{,}000$ accounts); asked only where a set is about to cost a real
query, it is free on exactly those blocks, and what it proves is banked as the entry's
floor.

Held at $20{,}000$ accounts and varied in $U$ instead, the \emph{definition's} cost is
linear in the underwriter count --- $0.35$\,s at $U = 20$, $2.02$\,s at $200$, $3.14$\,s
at $400$ --- and that linearity is what the witness removes: $4.2\times$ at $U = 10$
against $94\times$ at $U = 200$ in the table above, because the arm that grows with $U$ is
the one the commit path does not run. That matters because the underwriter role is open by
design, and an underwriter seat carrying live insured debt is a stock: four priced writes
seat it, on a young community inside the free allowance, and only settlement retires it
--- an expired obligation holds its arc indefinitely, and a declaration is floored at the
flow committed through it (Rem.~\ref{rem:uw-duration}). Three other shapes are available
and none is taken, because each changes what the ledger means rather than what it
computes: capping $U$ closes the role, a standing fee for a seat is rent
(Property~\ref{prop:no-rent}), and moving the audit off the commit path changes what a
commit guarantees.

Measured, \texttt{just cost}, on an Intel Core i9-14900HX under
\texttt{-{}-release} at a one-minute load average of $3.8$, which the harness prints
beside every figure; the harnesses are \texttt{crates/kernel/tests/cost.rs} and
\texttt{crates/state/tests/cost.rs}, and the community is $n/100$ founding underwriters
with eight stakes per account. A wall-clock figure without its generator and its machine
is not a measurement, and on a loaded box the same harness reads five times higher, which
is why the table above measures both arms on one state adjacent in time: a ratio taken
that way survives a box that is not idle, and a difference taken against a figure from
another run does not.

Both costs are bounded by \emph{community} size and never by network size, so a federation
of any extent costs each community exactly what its own membership costs.

\subsection{The state root}

Every block commits to a salted Merkle root over the ledger, with inclusion proofs for
any record. The ledger is divided into seven sections --- accounts, obligations,
proposals, validators, one stake row per account, the replay window bucketed by expiry,
and one scalar leaf for the rest. A stake row carries, per debtor, the arc's weight beside
both reservations on it --- what outstanding credit holds and what the seats do --- because
the two are facts about one arc and a section of their own would rehash a second tree for
the same writes. Leaves are length-framed in every variable-length component, commit their
own index, and use distinct leaf, inner and section domain separation; each section
commits its own leaf COUNT, once, at its root; odd levels promote the last node rather
than duplicating it.

Each of these closes a specific forgery. Unframed concatenation is not injective, so the
boundary between key and value can be slid and many distinct records share one preimage.
Uncommitted position lets a valid path be replayed at another index. An uncommitted
count lets a path be replayed against a tree of another size --- and it must be committed
somewhere other than the leaf, because which side each sibling sits on is a function of
the index alone, so a proof for leaf $0$ of a three-leaf section folds byte for byte to
the root of a four-leaf claim. Shared leaf and inner preimages let an inner node be
passed off as a leaf. Duplicate-last promotion makes two distinct trees share a root.
Binding the count at the section root rather than inside every leaf is also what makes
the tree incremental: a leaf survives its section growing, so seating an account rehashes
one leaf and its path instead of its whole section.

Leaves are salted from a per-chain secret fixed at genesis, so sibling hashes travelling
inside somebody else's inclusion proof do not expose preimages --- amounts and identifiers
occupy a small enough space to brute-force otherwise. The salt binds the record's value
through its digest and the EPOCH the record is committed in, so a disclosed salt unblinds
one epoch's value of one record and nothing else, no leaf hash survives an epoch boundary,
and within an epoch it does, which is the disclosure the cost paragraph states.

The commitment has two implementations held to each other: the definition is a pure
function of the ledger that rebuilds every leaf; what a validator runs patches the rows a
block wrote. The two are computed side by side after every transition in the state
harness and in the population runs of \S\ref{sec:verification}, and a disagreement is a
failure rather than a slower path --- the discipline the memoised audit is under, except
that this one decides a consensus value.

\subsection{Privacy model}

On the ledger a member is a pseudonymous handle. Charter policy may seal precise amounts
to the parties and validators, with other members seeing bucketed magnitudes. The
capacity computation is \emph{public} in structure: any observer holding the ledger
computes the same capacity a validator does, so the quantity is checkable rather than
announced. The stake graph is therefore visible in shape even where amounts are sealed,
and a community that regards its trading topology as sensitive should treat that as a
known cost.

\subsection{Workspace}

The economic kernel is a small pure library --- capacity, cascade arithmetic, constants,
an advisory risk score --- with no I/O, no clock and no state, so every function in it is
a deterministic map of its arguments. The ledger crate holds the state machine and its
invariants; the node crate holds consensus, storage and the network surfaces; the client
is a separate application that signs locally and reads a node over HTTP. Genesis values
live in the kernel and are cited above through generated macros, with a build gate that
fails if the two ever disagree.
