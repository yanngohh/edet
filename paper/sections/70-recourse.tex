\section{Recourse}
\label{sec:recourse}

What happens when an obligation fails depends on whether the community stood behind it.
An insured obligation is settled by \emph{substitution}: the underwriters whose supply
carried it become the creditor's debtors, and the defaulter's debt moves to them. Beside
it sit two optional instruments, an arbitration panel the parties consented to in advance
and the forfeiture of a member's operation bonds for abusing the write surface. An
uninsured obligation has no recourse machinery at all (\S\ref{sec:no-pool}). None of it
creates money.

\subsection{Substitution: what an underwriter actually pays}
\label{sec:substitution}

Corollary~\ref{cor:uw-bound} bounds an underwriter's loss; this section settles it. With
no external redemption there is no asset to hand over, so one constraint shapes the answer:

\begin{quote}
In a closed system, the only way anyone can pay is to become a debtor. ``You lose $x$''
can only mean ``you now owe $x$ of goods and services.''
\end{quote}

A default on an insured obligation is one event with two movements. In
\emph{substitution}, the creditor's claim lands on the underwriters, split by exactly what
each one's supply arc carried --- a lookup, since the obligation records which arcs it
drew and how much on each (\S\ref{sec:standing}) --- and each underwriter becomes an
ordinary debtor of the creditor, discharging the way anyone discharges anything. In
\emph{subrogation}, the defaulter's debt runs to the underwriters instead of to the
creditor, so a defaulter who cures repays the underwriter, and the creditor, already
whole, is not paid twice.

\begin{property}[Consent through declaration]
\label{prop:uw-consent}
Every discharge is authorised by the party who loses if it is wrong. Here that party is
the underwriter, who authorised it by declaring a supply: a declaration is not a number
but a signed, standing consent to inherit the debts of those the community's stakes
reach through the declarer, up to the declared amount.
\end{property}

The claim keeps the flow it held, with three consequences. The defaulter's standing stays
consumed, so paying a loss does not restore the member who caused it. The underwriter's
supply stays drawn until the defaulter repays them, so unpaid absorbed losses throttle
the declaration and \S\ref{sec:standing}'s withdrawal floor stops them walking out from
under it. And the reservation totals do not move: the pieces partition the original
reservation arc by arc.

A consented panel stays with the claim. Its terms record the parties and the amount they
were consented for, so the change of creditor does not reach them: the award is still the
buyer's remedy against the seller for non-delivery, minted between them, bounded by the
original amount, and never against an underwriter who was never asked. Dropping the panel
with the party would close the remedy in the one case arbitration exists for, a buyer who
withholds payment for non-delivery and defaults.

The substituted leg is \emph{uninsured}. Insuring it would draw on what others have put
behind that underwriter --- the loss walking one hop further out, the path contagion
\S\ref{sec:stability} refuses. If the underwriter defaults on it too, the creditor bears
the loss, visibly and attributably; without an external asset the system cannot do better
and must not pretend to. What substitution buys is that a supply declared by somebody who
cannot honour it fails in public.

\begin{remark}[An underwriter's loss is bounded; their time is not]
\label{rem:uw-duration}
Committed flow is released by the defaulter repaying and by nothing else --- not by the
underwriter satisfying the creditor, and not by time. An underwriter who has honoured a
substituted obligation in full therefore stays floored at the committed flow, unable to
withdraw the supply, exit the role or put a date on either, for as long as the defaulter
declines to cure. That is the throttle: releasing on a timer would let an underwriter
absorb a loss, wait, and insure the same amount over again. What is bounded is the
\emph{amount} (Corollary~\ref{cor:uw-bound}); the duration of a default is the defaulter's.
\emph{Extend} produces the same lock with no default at all, on the signatures of the
debtor and creditor alone --- but only within the \emph{insured horizon} of the claim's
acceptance, $\kConstInsuredHorizonEpochs$ epochs at genesis and governed
(\S\ref{sec:governance}): an acceptance or an extension past it books or leaves the claim
uninsured, the flow returns, and an underwriter's exposure in time on a performing claim is
bounded by a horizon the electorate --- the underwriters --- set in advance. The horizon
is measured from the acceptance, which a transfer and a routed successor inherit, never
from now, so no chain of extensions rolls it; keeping a claim insured longer is settling
and re-accepting it against the current cut.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Consented arbitration}

Parties may attach arbitration terms at acceptance: a panel of named arbiters, a quorum,
a window and an award ceiling, pinned there and immutable --- a channel cannot be opened,
widened or repointed afterwards by either party. Arbiters within the window each attest an
amount; at quorum the award is the \emph{median} of the attestations, minted once as an
ordinary obligation from the original \textbf{creditor} to the original \textbf{debtor},
the buyer's remedy for non-delivery: an obligation records that the debtor received value
and owes for it, and the dispute an award settles is whether the creditor delivered it.
The award is bounded three ways, all consented at acceptance --- the median, the agreed
ceiling, and the original amount.

The remedy is available in \emph{any} state of the original obligation, settled included,
and after substitution, when the row has become an underwriter's claim
(\S\ref{sec:substitution}): a buyer who has paid in full and received nothing is the case
it exists for, and gating it on the obligation still being open would close the channel
the moment it falls due.

\begin{property}[Median capture resistance]
\label{prop:median}
With a quorum of $q$ arbiters, an award is moved only by a coalition holding more than
$\lfloor q/2 \rfloor$ panel seats. A minority, however extreme its attestations, cannot
move the median.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[An award is bounded and uninsured]
\label{prop:additive}
An award is minted at most once per contract, never exceeds the original amount or the
agreed ceiling, and is \textbf{uninsured}: it reserves no flow, draws on no underwriter,
and substitutes nothing if it is never honoured --- it is worth exactly what
\S\ref{sec:medium} says any uninsured claim is worth. What it costs the loser is their
\emph{write} channel, since an operation bond is measured against headroom net of what the
member owes (Property~\ref{prop:write-floor}), and it nets against any future trade the
loser does with the winner, the ordinary way anything is paid in a mutual-credit
community.
\end{property}

An award is therefore worth less here than in a court. The alternative would draw on the
community's underwriters to satisfy a private dispute between two members: capacity
bounds what the community underwrites, and nobody underwrote the quarrel. The panel size
is capped at $\kConstNArb$, and arbiters may not be either party.

\subsection{Netting rings of defaults}
\label{sec:netting}

A sale nets before it creates: whatever the seller already owes the buyer extinguishes
first (\S\ref{sec:sale}, step 1). That is a cycle of length two, and nothing in the
argument depends on the length: in a ring of $A$ owing $B$ owing $C$ owing $A$, netting
the smallest of the three relieves each party of exactly as much debt as claim.

The epoch sweep does it with no signature, because every hop is in \emph{default}: every
party is already due, so nobody is paid early, nobody loses time value, and each is
relieved of a debt they did not pay against a claim they could not collect. Netting a live
obligation would hand its creditor an early payment they never agreed to take, so the
default condition is a rule rather than a heuristic, and a claim under an open arbitration
window belongs to the panel rather than to the sweep.

Each hop is an ordinary discharge and earns standing accordingly, capped as always by what
the creditor may confer: the debtor gave up a claim of their own worth exactly what they
were relieved of. Where a hop was insured, netting \emph{cures} it and the reservation
follows the debt down, which a default does not do on its own.

Substitution breaks a ring of insured defaults, and that is not a defect: an insured claim
moves onto the underwriters at default (\S\ref{sec:substitution}), so its creditor becomes
an underwriter and the cycle is a cycle no longer. What the sweep finds is therefore mostly
rings of \emph{uninsured} defaults, where nothing else was going to help: no underwriter
stands behind them and the creditors bear them alone.

Zero-priced and permissionless, it is bounded like every other crank: at most
$\kConstNettingMaxRing$ hops to a ring and $\kConstNettingMaxRingsPerEpoch$ rings to a
sweep. Every ring netted closes at least one hop and so destroys its own precondition, so a
sweep cannot find the same ring twice, and what is not reached this epoch is reached the
next.

\subsection{The uninsured tier, and why there is no pool}
\label{sec:no-pool}

The layer that suggests itself is a funded loss pool: members take a covenant --- a
per-event contribution cap, a committed balance and a ceiling on total commitment --- and a
creditor covered by it claims against a defaulted \emph{uninsured} obligation.
Assumption~\ref{ass:free-signatures} rules it out.

A covenant is funded by nothing the ledger can hold. A commitment reserves no flow and is
checked only against a ceiling the member declares for themselves, so it is a signature in
exactly the sense the assumption means: measured, a member holding $1.0$ of standing
pledges $10^9$, is displayed to every creditor as coverage, and mints $399.9998$ of a $400$
claim while an honest member's $500$ mints nothing --- an uninsured obligation the pledger
never honours. Capping the pledge does not repair it: a cap is checked once and not
maintained, so a member may pledge at their ceiling and then borrow the same capacity away
before any claim arrives. The only way to make a pledge funded is to \emph{reserve} flow,
which is the insured tier --- reserving against the claim actually at risk, at the moment
it is taken, bounded by the cut, subrogated when it fails. A pool cannot be made sound
without becoming substitution, and the measurement in \S\ref{sec:security} is the same
fact from the other side: a mutual pool funded from inside re-labels the seed instead of
adding to it.

\begin{remark}[The uninsured tier is borne alone, and that is what earns standing]
\label{rem:uninsured-alone}
A creditor who lends beyond a debtor's capacity bears the loss --- \S\ref{sec:medium}'s
asymmetry, unqualified. Settlement is the only way the stake graph grows
(Remark~\ref{rem:only-settlement}), so a creditor's own risk on a first trade is what
confers standing on a stranger. Socialising it would make standing manufacturable: a
ring covenanting for one another could trade inside itself, default, and extract every
honest pledge for the price of restitution it never honours.
\end{remark}

Mutual aid needs no transition. A member who chooses to carry a neighbour's loss issues the
injured creditor an obligation, or nets it against their own trade, at the moment the loss
is real --- funded when it is used, targeted at the loss that happened, and signed by
somebody with the capacity to mean it. What the pool added was a number the ledger could
not keep.

\subsection{Operation bonds}

Writes are feeless: no gas, no fee token, no revenue to anyone
(Property~\ref{prop:no-rent}). Spam is priced instead by \emph{bonds}: a transition beyond
a member's free allowance of $\kConstBondFreeAllowance$ per epoch encumbers headroom equal
to the bond unit $\kConstBondUnitFraction \cdot \vbase$, which returns after
$\kConstBondReleaseEpochs$ epoch unless forfeited. The headroom it is measured against is
not the declared supply but what the community's external seed reaches the member for
(Property~\ref{prop:write-floor}).

A bond is reserved headroom, never a balance: nothing in it is owed to anyone and every
entry returns on schedule. Only sustained exhaustion --- being denied by the gate in
$\kConstBondForfeitEpochs$ consecutive epochs without returning under the ceiling --- makes
bonds forfeitable, and a forfeited bond is recorded against the member rather than paid to
anyone.

\begin{property}[A forfeiture is a reservation that never returns]
\label{prop:forfeit}
Forfeiture removes the encumbered headroom permanently: the amount is recorded against the
member and subtracted from their bond headroom from then on. Nothing is minted, nobody is
owed it, and no position anywhere improves, so the sanction is enforceable by arithmetic
and still collects no rent (Property~\ref{prop:no-rent}). The way back is the model's own:
a member whose standing grows beyond what was taken has a write channel again.
\end{property}

Two exemptions keep the sanction from being absorbing. The free allowance still applies,
and the recovery path --- discharge, cure, transfer, exit, and reducing a supply --- is
priced at zero, so a member who has lost its headroom can still pay what it owes, provided
somebody can sign for the payee. A discharge needs the creditor's signature, and a creditor
who has lost their keys or stopped answering would otherwise make their debtor a permanent
defaulter --- an uninsured default substitutes nothing, and an open default holds the
allowance and the exit. So a threshold of the creditor's \emph{guardians} may sign a
discharge in the creditor's stead: they already hold the power to rotate the creditor's key
and sign as them, so this adds no trust the creditor had not placed. A creditor who
registered no guardians and answers nothing leaves a debt nobody can discharge, a fact
about that creditor the wallet states when a counterparty has none. The same exemption
keeps the \emph{governance} sanction from being absorbing: a suspended account's headroom
is zero by status rather than by exhaustion, and a suspended underwriter may still lower
what they stand behind. A refusal for want of a budget that was never charged is reported
as its own condition, because the remedies differ --- one waits for a bond to release and
the other cannot --- and because the exhaustion refusal is the evidence the forfeiture
crank counts, which must not accumulate against a member for asking.

The allowance is not what carries a newcomer: it goes only to accounts with something to
lose (Property~\ref{prop:write-floor}), or $N$ free keys would carry $N$ allowances. What
carries a newcomer is the counterparty, whose bonded first trade seats the row and holds a
seat on their reach for its life (Remark~\ref{rem:friction}, Property~\ref{prop:seat}).
